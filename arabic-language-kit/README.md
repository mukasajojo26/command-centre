# Arabic Correspondence Starter Kit

This kit is for a Safety and Security Manager in an Abu Dhabi hotel who has to correspond with the authorities in Arabic without speaking it.

## Files

| File | What it is |
|---|---|
| `Arabic-Letter-Templates.docx` | 15 letter templates. Each has an English approval copy and an Arabic version to send, plus a risk tier, a review rule and usage notes. |
| `Correspondence-Tracker-and-Glossary.xlsx` | Correspondence tracker with automatic overdue and due-soon alerts, a dashboard, a glossary of 115 terms (English, Arabic, transliteration), a template index, and UAE emergency numbers with phone phrases. |

## Before first use

1. **Get one paid review.** A certified Arabic translator checks the Arabic in all 15 templates and the glossary. Record their name and the date in the `Templates` and `Glossary` sheets. Until this is done, treat every Arabic line as a draft.
2. **Check official names.** Compare the authority names in the glossary with the letterhead on the last letter each authority sent you.
3. **Confirm processes.** Some notifications and renewals, such as system impairments and fire safety certificates, may have to go through an online government portal. Use these templates as cover letters where that applies.
4. Put the templates on your hotel letterhead and delete the example rows in the Tracker.

## Review rule by risk tier

- **Low:** use the template, check every number and date by eye, and send.
- **Medium:** two-engine AI check plus back-translation, then a 5 to 10 minute read by an Arabic speaker.
- **High:** full review by a qualified Arabic speaker before sending. No exceptions.
